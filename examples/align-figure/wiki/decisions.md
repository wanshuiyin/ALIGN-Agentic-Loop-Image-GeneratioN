## D-16 · 2026-09-05 · A brush stroke is one filled outline that tapers into its head

Reviewer (round 3, tell 1, primitive): "The long seal-to-wiki shaft shows regular segmentation; the blue dashes repeat the same little wedge; the chevrons look mechanically attached." The user, before the round (D-15): 箭头等宽无起收. Both are right and both name the same construction: `brush()` cuts the path into ≈ 8-px butt-capped pieces with width quantised to 0.25 px and alpha to 10/255, so the shaft bands visibly at 2×, the ±10 % ink load never shows at 3–5 px, and the shaft runs at full width to the apex where `head()` sits on top of it. Rule from here: **every arrow, head and dash is one filled polygon** — the two offset edges of its width profile, closed — never a chain of stroked pieces. The profile: a 顿 at the 起笔 (a short swell, wider than the body, over the first few percent), a body that breathes enough to read at 3 px, a 收笔 that tapers over the last tenth *into the apex*; the chevron's two strokes start at that apex with their own swell and taper outward, their inner edges meeting the shaft's tail, so shaft and head are one gesture. Dashes are short filled tapers whose lengths vary at build (seeded), not one wedge repeated. `rule`-style lines (panel outlines, ticks, the scrub bar, the ledger rule) keep their constant width and are not the tell. In the SVG each stroke is one `<path>` with a fill — v3's 2750 pieces become a few hundred paths, which the paper's editor will prefer. Widths and swell ratios are set from the band-2 render at 2× before the primitive goes across the figure; the four weights and colours by role (5 / 3 / 3-dashed / 2 — see D-17) do not change.
